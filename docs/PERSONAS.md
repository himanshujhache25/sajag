# The four people we build for

Every screen decision is checked against these four.

## Rameshwar Prasad, 61, Gorakhpur

Retired school clerk. Reads Hindi comfortably, English slowly and with effort.
An Android phone with 2 GB of memory and 4G that drops in his lane. Last
Tuesday a number he does not know added him to a group called "VIP Premium
Research". The messages promise pakka munafa and show screenshots of other
members' profits. He does not sign up for apps that ask for a password, and he
will not type a long message. He will press a big button and he will speak. He
wants one thing from us: tell him whether to walk away, in words he uses
himself, and tell him honestly when we do not know.

## Aman Yadav, 24, Jhansi

Trades options on his phone, mostly at night, mostly after a loss. The savings
went first, then he funded the account with an instant loan because the app
offered it in two taps. He is in four Telegram tip channels. He does not think
he has a problem and he will close anything that lectures him. He might open a
60-second pause screen at 11:40 pm if it does not shame him, and he will use the
first-hour plan the day something goes wrong.

## Kavita Devi, 46, Satna

Her husband's demat account came to her after he died. She has the statements in
a folder and no idea which form does what. She has never heard of SCORES. She is
not afraid of the market; she is afraid of filling a form wrongly and losing
what is there. She needs plain words, one step at a time, and a printed page she
can carry to the bank.

## Priya Rao, 29, Bengaluru

Software engineer. She sets up her mother's phone over a video call, two states
away. She wants her mother to have a way to check a message without calling her
at midnight, and she wants to be told when something looks wrong. She will not
accept an app that reads her mother's messages, and she will notice if one does.
So the alert is sent by her mother, with her own thumb, or not at all.
